# Split the 1,432-line `deploy` job into stages, or decide deliberately that it stays one job and record why.
REPO: samaydlette.com
STATUS: QUEUED            # QUEUED | IN_PROGRESS | PARKED | DONE | FAILED
ACCEPTANCE: `make check` passes, plus: a full deploy runs green end to end and the served `/.well-known/ksi-signal.json` carries that run's commit and a fresh `emitted_at` (the publish-freshness invariant is the thing most likely to break under a job split, so verify it at the edge with `curl`, not from the build log); `actionlint` parses the workflow; the reconciliation gate still runs **before** the publish step and still fails closed; and the `prod` environment approval still gates every path that reaches `terraform apply` — prove that by checking the job that carries `environment: prod` is the one that applies, not merely one that precedes it.
OUT OF SCOPE: Changing what any step does, which artifacts are built, the order they are built in, or any gate's semantics. Removing or weakening the reconciliation gate, the OPA gate, or the environment approval to make a split easier — `CLAUDE.md` names that as the one thing never to do, and a split that moves the gate after the publish is a catastrophic regression wearing a refactor's clothing. Converting steps to a reusable workflow or composite action in the same change (that is a further step, and it should follow a plain split). Retiring the `REBUILD_STATE` import steps — backlog/006 owns those, and doing both at once makes the diff unreviewable. `docs/poam.md`, `website/.well-known/` contents, `infrastructure/`.
LANDMINES: **Every step in this job shares a filesystem, and that is load-bearing.** Measured 2026-08-25 on `main` @ 4223377: `.github/workflows/deploy-with-opa.yml` is 2,119 lines and the `deploy` job spans L503-L1934 — **1,432 lines**, roughly 57 steps. The generators write their output into `infrastructure/` and later steps read it from there: the KSI signal builder writes `ksi-signal.json`, then the SSP, POA&M, VDR, IIW, SCuBA, SDR, CPO, OCR and CDS builders read it and write beside it, then the reconciliation gate reads the whole directory at once, then the signing and publish steps consume it. Splitting on a step boundary means every one of those hand-offs becomes an `upload-artifact`/`download-artifact` pair, and each one is a chance to publish a *stale* file — which is exactly the failure invariant (f) exists to catch and which `CLAUDE.md` records as having bitten before ("a stale serve is a bug, not a cache quirk"). Second trap: `id-token: write` for cosign keyless signing is granted per job; every new job that signs needs it, and a job that has it but should not is a privilege widening. Third: the `prod` environment approval currently gates this single job, so *all* of apply-and-publish sits behind one human decision; split carelessly and you can end up with the approval gating only the apply while the publish runs unattended in a later job. Fourth: the two `REBUILD_STATE` blocks (297 lines, backlog/006) are pure noise in the middle of this job and land it first if both are going to happen — the split is much easier to read against a 1,135-line job.

Diagnosed 2026-08-25 during the cleanup review (PR #306), recorded as out of scope there.

This is the lowest-priority of the four cleanup findings and the only one where "do nothing" is a legitimate answer. The job is long because it is a genuinely serial pipeline whose steps share a working directory; that is a real constraint, not an accident of style, and the current arrangement makes the stale-artifact class of bug *structurally impossible* within the job. A split trades that guarantee for readability and for the ability to re-run one stage.

So the deliverable may be a decision rather than a diff. If the answer is "it stays one job", say so in a comment at the top of the job naming the artifact-passing hazard as the reason, and close this entry as DONE — a recorded decision is worth more than an open question that gets re-litigated every time someone new reads the file.

If it is split, the natural seam is not step-by-step but at the two points where the filesystem hand-off is already explicit:

1. **plan + gate** (through the OPA compliance check) — already a separate job today.
2. **apply + generate** — everything that writes into `infrastructure/`, ending at the reconciliation gate. The gate must stay in the same job as the generators, so it reads the files they actually wrote.
3. **sign + publish + verify** — consumes the gated artifact set as one uploaded bundle, so there is exactly one hand-off to get right instead of forty.

That is three jobs, one artifact hand-off, and the gate still runs before anything is published. Any split finer than that multiplies the hand-offs without buying anything.
