Derive from something that is actually stable: the `poam-id` prop is the natural key, but confirm every item has one before relying on it, because an item without a `poam-id` would collapse two entries onto one UUID. `stable_uuid` in `scripts/build-oscal-ssp.py` L890 is the working implementation; it belongs in `scripts/_common.py` alongside the existing `sid(ns, *parts)` helper rather than being copy-pasted a third time.

Diagnosed 2026-08-25 during the cleanup review (PR #306), where it was recorded as out of scope precisely because it alters a published artifact.

The failure this closes is not cosmetic. Every deploy currently republishes a POA&M whose 33 item identifiers are all new, which means:

1. The signed artifact and its provenance attestation churn on every run even when the compliance posture has not moved at all, so a consumer diffing yesterday's POA&M against today's sees 37 spurious changes and no signal.
2. The OSCAL `uuid` — the field the format designates as the stable handle for an item — cannot be used as one by anybody. An external tool tracking "has POAM-018 changed?" has to know to ignore the uuid and key on a FedRAMP-namespace prop instead.
3. It quietly contradicts the reproducibility claim the rest of the pipeline makes. Every other artifact derives from one inventory `signal_id` and is meant to be a pure function of it; this one is not, and nothing says so.

Suggested order:

1. Move `stable_uuid` out of `build-oscal-ssp.py` into `scripts/_common.py`, unchanged, with its namespace still passed in — the module already holds `sid(ns, *parts)` for exactly this and the two should look alike.
2. Pick the derivation names and write them down in a comment: item UUIDs from the `poam-id`, party and system from fixed literal names. Assert every item has a `poam-id` before you rely on it.
3. Replace the four `uuid4()` sites. Diff the before and after POA&M and confirm the *only* changed lines are uuid values and the timestamp.
4. Add the two-build stability test.
