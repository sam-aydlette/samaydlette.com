Access logging and your privacy

FedRAMP asks trust centers to log access to certification data and keep summaries of it (CDS-TRC-ACL). This is what this site logs, and what it does not.

  • What is logged. For each request to the site, the CDN records the date and time, the edge location and country it was served from, the method, the path, the response status, the bytes sent and whether it came from cache. It does not record your IP address, user agent, referrer, cookies or query string: those fields are switched off at the edge, so they are never stored.
  • Why. To show, in aggregate, how the certification data under /.well-known/ and this trust center are used, as FedRAMP requires.
  • How long. The raw records are deleted after 90 days. Only monthly counts (per artifact, per response class, per country) are kept, for 13 months, and published at /.well-known/access-summary.json (readable version). Requests to other pages of the site are not counted.
  • Who can see the raw records. Only the operator and the deploy pipeline's read-only role, in an encrypted bucket that is not public.
  • Agencies. An agency is identified only by what it states in its own access request. Nothing is inferred from network addresses, which are not logged.
  • Your own records. You can ask for the summaries and for the records of your own requests, identified by the times and paths you give us (the records hold nothing that identifies a requester). Write to the security contact listed in the certification package.
